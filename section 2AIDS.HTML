HOW AIDS IS TRANSMITTED

Transmission of AIDS


  • Blood Contact: Direct contact with infected blood can spread HIV. This may happen through sharing needles, blood transfusion, or accidental cuts involving infected blood In which people are advised to have personal tools like ;Razor blades and others to avoid risk of infections due to sharing of these items which may be harmful in day to day life ,we are supposed to be very careful in not just touching peoples blood when hand barely we have to take needed precautions even when helping them with injuries.

  • Sharing Injection Equipment: Drug users who share injection equipment are at high risk of HIV transmission because infected blood may remain on the needles,which may lead to get shared the virus in the contaminated equipments when shared,Also in hospitals doctors are highly required to be carefull in not using the same syringes for multiple patients where sharp equipments are also required to be sterilized for safety and for killing germs.

  • Mother-to-Baby Transmission: A pregnant woman with HIV can transmit the virus to her child during pregnancy, labor, delivery, or breastfeeding in which the women are required to have and to take proper measures in order to keep safe thier babies until the delivery period and to maintain good health to the baby .

  • Healthcare Exposure: Healthcare workers may become infected through accidental needle-stick injuries or contact with infected blood if proper safety measures are not followed in which the heailth care workers like nurses are required to have very proper measures to protect themselves in every day working conditions.

  • Traditional Practices: Certain traditional practices involving cutting the skin with unsterilized instruments may contribute to HIV transmission when the instruments are contaminated societies are required to fight these negative practises which may be affecting various individuals including spread of AIDS and other various infections that may generally affect their health conditions.
  • Sexual Transmission: HIV is commonly transmitted through unprotected sexual intercourse with an infected person. The virus enters through body fluids during sexual contact.
